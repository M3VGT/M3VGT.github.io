01 THE IDEA

Let morphology carry reusable task structure.

A variable-geometry truss can move its wheels, raise its chassis, and redistribute support by changing the lengths of its load-bearing members. The same flexibility creates a difficult coordination problem: a small set of task objectives must be mapped to 21 coupled actuators.

GeoTrussRover solves one source traversal, extracts four contact-semantic coordination primitives, and projects them onto the physically feasible morphology set of a new step height. Whole-body feedback then corrects member and wheel errors without forcing the controller to remain inside the primitive subspace.

01

Store coordination

Consecutive feasible keyframes encode which members lengthen and shorten together during each support phase.

02

Adapt only what changes

Physical projection preserves the source coordination while correcting height-dependent closure, clearance, support, and stroke constraints.

03

Correct in full space

A primitive-guided QP treats the adapted path as a soft reference and retains local freedom for every member and wheel.

02 METHOD

One source solve.
Many related traversals.

The offline path stores contact-conditioned morphology changes. Online feedback executes the transferred path at 50 Hz.

03 CONTACT-SEMANTIC PRIMITIVES

A traversal becomes a reusable motion cycle.

The primitive sequence follows the contact semantics of the task rather than elapsed time. Each phase advances only after its target morphology and support condition are reached.

  1. P1
    Front-right liftestablish the first elevated support
  2. P2
    Front-left liftseat the front axle on the upper surface
  3. P3
    Chassis elevationclear the edge while preserving support
  4. P4
    Wheelbase contractionrecover the rear axle and transfer the body

Across heights, P1 and P2 change comparatively little. P3 and especially P4 move farther on the morphology manifold, identifying the phases where projection or local recomputation matters most.
